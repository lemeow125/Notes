# I show you perspective
I'm a generalist by trade. Hand me a problem that doesn't have a clean owner yet and I'll work out what it actually is before deciding what to do about it.

# The diagnosis comes first

You describe the problem. I ask questions until the shape of it is clear.

One conversation, about an hour, no charge. If clarity is enough for you to solve it yourself, take it and go — that's success for me.

If you walk away knowing more about your own system than you did an hour ago, the conversation did its job. I'd rather that than talk you into work you didn't need.

If it turns out I'm the right person to build the thing, we talk about that next. If I'm not, I'll tell you and point at what I'd look for instead.

# If I hire you, what will you actually own?
The practices. Documentation that matches the system, coding standards people actually follow, runbooks someone has read this year, the ten minutes of unblocking someone at 4pm.

Someone already owns the platform? Good. That's a division of labor, not a problem.

I work the cracks. Hand me what nobody has time for and it stops being nobody's.


# What I'm actually good at

- **Making a messy system legible**. Monitoring, alerting, runbooks, documentation — the boring work that means the next person doesn't have to reverse-engineer your platform from logs.

- **Breadth is what makes depth cheap.** Point me at a scope and I'll go the whole way down. Four systems, four domains, no shared stack — the patterns transfer when the tooling doesn't. A quarter to own the unfamiliar, and documentation left behind instead of dependence.

- **Being the person people want to work with**. Across every team I've been on, the pattern is the same: I end up as the one that empowers people. I am amiable get the work done, and am a team player, it's just what the references say [unprompted](https://www.linkedin.com/in/keannu-christian-bernasol-843464265/details/recommendations).

- **Cybersecurity pipelines that can't drop data**. Multi-tenant ingestion at scale, provider API integrations, retries, backpressure, cost control. I've run one that ingested tens of millions of security alerts a year from twenty-plus EDR/MDR vendors.

- **Incident Triage**. I've been the point of contact on a cross-team ingestion failure spanning four teams on-call. I know what that weekend feels like and what to do during it.

- **Azure & on-prem platforms**. Function Apps, Container Apps, docker compose, CI/CD that people trust enough to deploy on a Friday, regardless of your size.

# What I'm not

- **Not an agency**. I put quality in work, a small number of things at a time.

- **Student Coding Work**. If you're a student or a junior, I will instead advise free-of-charge and give you direction. I will not build what you can learn out of.

- **Extremely Small-Scale Work**. If you just need a quick, one-off script—like "write us a web scraper in 3 days"—that's not where my time adds value. You're much better off using AI for that and saving your budget for problems that actually need an engineer.

# Work

I'm your average Joe when it comes to schedules—available Monday through Friday, 3 AM on-call rotations only when necessary.

- **Timezone flexibility.** Based in the Philippines, and I regularly align with UK, EU, and PH working hours without a hitch. Whether we need live overlap for pairing and triage or async handoffs, we'll make the clock work.
- **Pacing that fits the problem.** I don't only do massive, full-time builds. If you need steady, smaller-scale progress—a few hours a week, a focused audit, or fractional help—we can negotiate a cadence that makes sense for your budget and backlog.
