title: Nobody needs a hand-carved pallet
slug: /nobody-needs-a-hand-carved-pallet

I’ve shipped things I knew were wrong in the back of my head. Not out of laziness or some sweatshop free-for-all, just schedules and keeping food on the table when you’re on the inside. That’s part of the deal.

Most people don’t want to stare at the whole blueprint anyway. They need the direction a factory gives—a clear line, a set of rules, someone else holding the map. Factories aren’t a moral failure; they’re just built for people who don't want to carry the whole blueprint.

When you step toward the workshop side, the math gets lonely. Fewer people come looking for craft, so you price for what it actually costs, and that price filters out almost everyone—even folks who already had a foot in the door. When they walk away, I don’t keep score. Maybe six months later it breaks and they see why. Maybe it doesn’t break at all and they win anyway. None of it is black and white.

Let the line run. It feeds the city, and plenty of people on it clock out at five and build something of their own on Saturday.

The line ships pallets. Workshops build tables. Neither has to pretend to be the other. Nobody asked for a hand-carved pallet.
