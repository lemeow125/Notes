
Work is a transaction. It isn't charity, and it isn't a crime.

I've had to tell myself that repeatedly. I give things away to students and there's less tension there — nobody's watching. But a transaction can never hold the trust you see when nothing is being bought. I write this to remind myself, not to convince you.

## Why passion makes it worse

If you do the work anyway, the price stops being about the work and starts being about you. Charging for something you love reads as a confession. "For the experience" is a launch pad, not a bank.

And when it runs out, you're burnt out on the only thing you loved.

You can't put a price on a person — which is exactly why the number has to sit on the work and nowhere else.

## What a transaction needs

- **What's sold.** Hours, a deliverable, a result. Same words on both sides.
- **The number and its reason.** _It's X because Y_, where Y points at the work.
- **Failure terms.** Late, wrong, abandoned: who decides, by when, who pays.

Trust builds around those. It can't be load-bearing — people are ambiguous, not through any fault of their own, and no document fixes it.

## The part I'd rather skip

In a perfect world this would all be charity, or good of heart, or passion. Raising kids isn't transactional, it's a commitment. I wish that were true here. It can't be.

I'd rather skip the fluff. I hate it too. I hope you know that.
